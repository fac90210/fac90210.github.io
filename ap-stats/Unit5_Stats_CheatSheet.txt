# 1 SCATTERPLOTS
x-axis = EXPLANATORY (independent, predictor)
y-axis = RESPONSE (dependent, outcome)
Each point = 1 individual. Univariate=1 var, bivariate=2 vars.
DESCRIBE: DUFS + CONTEXT
D Direction: positive / negative / none
U Unusual: outliers, gaps, clusters
F Form: linear / curved (quad, exp, log)
S Strength: weak / moderate / strong
Template: "There is a strong, positive, linear relationship between [x] and [y]. [Point] is a possible outlier."
Ex Cigarettes/wk vs doctor visits: strong, pos, linear (r=0.926, r^2=.857, yhat=1.307+0.0916x), no clear outliers.
Ex Star Wars: x=screen time, y=rank. Weak, negative, linear, R2-D2 (410,7) & Padme (210,12) unusual. r=-0.380
# 2 UNUSUAL POINTS
OUTLIER: extreme y / large residual (vertical).
HIGH LEVERAGE: extreme x, far from xbar (horizontal).
INFLUENTIAL: removing it changes slope/int/r a lot. Usually high leverage pts that DON'T follow the trend.
Pt far left low ON trend: HL yes, Out no, Infl no (strengthens r).
Pt left & high above trend (x in range): HL no, Out yes, Infl small.
Pt far left & high, away from cluster: HL yes, Out yes, Infl yes.
Vertical outlier in middle: slope ~same, int shifts up/down a bit, r DECREASES.
Horizontal outlier off trend: slope & int change a LOT, r decreases. Horizontal (high leverage) outliers have the biggest impact on the LSRL.
Rubik's (14,8.4) above line at right end: slope less steep (closer to 0), y-int decreases, r weaker (closer to 0).
# 3 CORRELATION r
r = strength & direction of LINEAR relationship.
Facts: -1<=r<=1. Near +-1 = strong. Near 0 = weak/none.
r is ONLY for linear. No units; changing units doesn't change r. Swapping x & y doesn't change r. Strongly affected by outliers.
Strong r does NOT mean linear (check resid plot) and does NOT mean causation!
INTERPRET r: "The linear relationship between [x] and [y] is [strong], [positive]."
Rough: |r|<.3 weak, .3-.7 moderate, >.7 strong.
r = +-sqrt(r^2), sign = sign of slope.
Point on the trend beyond data (bungee 12 bands) -> r increases (.987->.992).
Typo outlier (85 instead of 58) -> r decreases (.987->.913).
Ex spider bites r=.806: strong pos linear relationship between letters in winning word & spider deaths. r^2=.65 -> 65% of variation in deaths explained by linear rel. w/ letters. NOT causation (coincidence/lurking var).
# 4 LSRL
yhat = a + bx   (a = y-int, b = slope)
b = r(sy/sx)     a = ybar - b(xbar)
Line always goes through (xbar, ybar).
Least squares = minimizes the SUM of the SQUARED RESIDUALS.
SLOPE: "For each additional 1 [unit x], the PREDICTED [y] increases/decreases by [b] [units]."
Y-INT: "When [x] = 0, the PREDICTED [y] is [a]." Say if it makes sense.
Always use yhat (predicted), and define x & y in context.
EXTRAPOLATION = predicting outside the x range of data -> unreliable!
Ex Rubik's: b=-.793(1.20/4.47)=-0.213, a=7.71-(-.213)(8)=9.41 -> yhat=9.41-0.213x
Ex Michael yhat=20+160x: each extra mile -> predicted +160 cal. y-int 20 cal at 0 mi: no real meaning. 5 mi: 20+160(5)=820. resid=760-820=-60 (burned 60 fewer than predicted). Marathon 26.2 mi = extrapolation, don't trust.
Ex bungee (no 5): yhat=25.33+7.46x. x=5 -> 62.6 cm. Actual 64 -> resid +1.4 (above). x=20 -> 174.6, less trust (extrapolation). y-int: 0 bands -> pred 25.3cm. slope: each band -> pred +7.46cm.
# 5 RESIDUALS
RESIDUAL = ACTUAL - PREDICTED = y - yhat  (R = A - P)
+ resid: point ABOVE line (underpredicted)
- resid: point BELOW line (overpredicted)
INTERPRET: "The actual [y] was [|resid|] above/below the predicted value for [x]=#."
Sum (and mean) of residuals = 0.
# 6 RESIDUAL PLOTS
Plot x (or yhat) vs residual.
GOOD (linear OK): random scatter, no pattern, around 0.
BAD (not linear): curve/U-shape pattern, or fan (spread grows).
"Because there is no leftover pattern in the residual plot, a linear model is appropriate."
Ex Monster yhat=-20.5958+2.7938x (x=yrs after 2000): 2010 pred 7.34 res -3.66; 2014 pred 18.52 res -5.13; 2022 pred 40.87 res +4.14. Resid U-shape (+,-,+) -> linear NOT appropriate (curved, exp better). r^2=.943: 94.3% of variation in stock price explained by linear rel. w/ years. Above pred: 2006,08,20,22. Below: 2010-18.
Ex Fuel: yhat=1.151+1.469(city mpg). Strong pos linear, no outliers. Resid plot random -> linear appropriate.
# 7 r^2 and s
r^2 = coefficient of determination.
INTERPRET r^2: "[r^2]% of the variation in [y] is explained by the linear relationship with [x]."
(= % improvement in SSE using LSRL vs ybar)
s = std dev of residuals: "The actual [y] is typically about [s] [units] away from the predicted [y] using the LSRL with [x]."
# 8 COMPUTER OUTPUT
Predictor  Coef     SE   T   P
Constant   a=y-int  (ignore T,P,SE)
[x name]   b=slope
S = s (typical resid)   R-sq = r^2
IGNORE R-sq(adj).
Ex: Const 3.371, Age 2.1143, s=.971, R-sq 95.4% -> yhat=3.371+2.1143(age), r=+sqrt(.954)=0.977
Ex Star Wars: Const 10.829, Screen -0.015, S=4.027, R-sq=14.5%
 rank-hat = 10.829-0.015(screen min)
 R2-D2: 10.829-.015(410)=4.68; resid=7-4.68=2.32
 r^2: 14.5% of variation in rank explained by linear rel. w/ screen time.
 r = -sqrt(.145) = -0.381 (neg since slope neg)

# SENTENCE FRAMES
r: "The correlation of __ indicates a (strong/mod/weak), (pos/neg) linear relationship between [y] and [x]."
r^2: "__% of the variability in [y] can be explained by the linear association with [x]."
slope: "For every increase of one [x unit], there is an average (inc/dec) in predicted [y] of [b] [units]."
y-int: "When [x] is 0 [units], I would predict [y] to be [a]."
